\chapter{Quantum mechanics of the neck}\label{ch:jt}

\begin{questionbox}
Is there a quantum theory of the neck that can actually be solved? Yes. Near the neck, four-dimensional gravity with the Yang--Mills field reduces to Jackiw--Teitelboim gravity in two dimensions, whose quantum theory is known exactly: its boundary dynamics is the Schwarzian, and its density of states is a hyperbolic sine. What the dumbbell adds is one unstable field, $W$.
\end{questionbox}

\section{From four dimensions to two}

Write a spherically symmetric metric as $-N^2dt^2+A^2dx^2+R^2d\Omega^2$ and set $\Phi=R^2$. The four-dimensional curvature splits as
\[
NAR^2R_4=NA\Bigl(\Phi R_2+2+\frac{(\nabla\Phi)^2}{2\Phi}\Bigr)-2\,\mathrm{Div},
\]
where $R_2$ is the curvature of the two-dimensional part and Div is a total derivative (\lean{reduction\_identity}, \lean{div\_total}). The Yang--Mills field at $W=0$ contributes $F^2=2/R^4$, and after integrating over the sphere the action becomes
\[
S_2=\frac1{4G}\int\sqrt{-g_2}\Bigl[\Phi R_2+\frac{(\nabla\Phi)^2}{2\Phi}+U(\Phi)\Bigr],\qquad U(\Phi)=2-\frac{2\ell^2}\Phi
\]
(\lean{magnetic\_F2}, \lean{potential\_from\_YM}).

The neck is the point where $U$ vanishes: $\Phi=\ell^2$, and then $R_2=-U'(\ell^2)=-2/\ell^2$, two-dimensional anti-de~Sitter space of radius $\ell$ (\lean{neck\_exact}, \lean{fam\_jt\_U0}, \lean{fam\_jt\_slope}).

\section{Jackiw--Teitelboim gravity}

Expand around the neck, $\Phi=\ell^2(1+\phi)$. To first order in $\phi$,
\[
S_2\approx\frac{\ell^2}{4G}\int\sqrt{-g}\,R+\frac{\ell^2}{4G}\int\sqrt{-g}\,\phi\Bigl(R+\frac2{\ell^2}\Bigr),
\]
and the remainder is given exactly (\lean{jt\_expansion}, \lean{fam\_jt\_coeff}). The second term is the Jackiw--Teitelboim action. The first is topological; over a disc it gives the coefficient $\pi\ell^2/G$, the entropy of the extremal black hole (\lean{fam\_jt\_S0}, \lean{ground\_entropy}).

The JT dilaton on the neck solves its three field equations with $\phi=A\sin(t/\ell)+\cos(t/\ell)(B\cosh(z/\ell)+C\sinh(z/\ell))$ (\lean{jt\_neck\_solution}). In the quantum theory the boundary of $\mathrm{AdS}_2$ carries the Schwarzian mode, with energy $E=2\pi^2CT^2$ and entropy linear in $T$ (\lean{schwarzian\_energy}, \lean{schwarzian\_entropy}, \lean{schwarzian\_heat\_capacity}). With the exact density of states $\rho(E)\propto\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{2CE})$ the inverse temperature is $1/T=4\pi^2C\coth x/x$ with $x=2\pi\sqrt{2CE}$, which differs from the saddle point only by exponentially small terms (\lean{micro\_exact\_inv\_T}, \lean{micro\_exact\_bound}).

\section{What the dumbbell adds}

On the JT background the field $W$ has $m^2\ell^2=-1$. The equation $\Delta(\Delta-1)=-1$ for its scaling dimension has no real solution, $\Delta=(1\pm i\sqrt3)/2$ (\lean{delta\_roots}, \lean{fam\_no\_real\_dim}, \lean{neck\_mode\_below\_BF}). The quantum neck is therefore JT gravity plus one field below the BF bound. Chapter~20 showed that a Higgs field can lift it above the bound.

\begin{asidebox}{Why this matters now}
JT gravity and the Schwarzian are central to current work on near-extremal black holes, where they explain the low-temperature behaviour of the density of states. A complex scaling dimension is a sharper effect than the finite smoothness found recently for generic extremal horizons. Whether the $C^1$ but not $C^2$ junction of the dumbbell is related to these scaling dimensions is an open question that this book raises but does not answer.
\end{asidebox}

\begin{standsbox}
The reduction to JT gravity and its Schwarzian quantisation are known. The reduction with the $SU(2)$ field, the exact remainder, and the identification of the unstable $W$ field are checked here.
\end{standsbox}
