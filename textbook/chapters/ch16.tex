\chapter{Static Solutions}
\label{ch:static}

\begin{usesbox}
\textbf{This chapter uses:} the spherical Einstein tensor and the Yang--Mills stress (\cref{thm:einD,thm:ymstress}); the spin-connection condition $w=r'$ and the homogeneous solutions (\cref{thm:spinwitten,thm:threesol}); the Tolman universe and the Bertotti--Robinson throat (\cref{thm:tolman,thm:br}).\\
\textbf{It provides:} a complete classification of static solutions with $w=r'$, which leaves only the throat and flat space, so that no static solution connects the two; and, allowing a cosmological constant, a ``map of smoothness'': static throats cannot have a waist, cylinders exist exactly for $\Lambda\le1/(4\ell^2)$, there is an exact static closed universe with Yang--Mills field, and inside the throat the Yang--Mills perturbation violates the Breitenlohner--Freedman bound.
\end{usesbox}

The Tolman universe is dynamical; the throat is static. The natural first question about joining them is whether a static configuration can interpolate between a region that looks like the round sphere and a region that looks like the throat. In EYM theory the answer is no. This chapter proves it under the spin-connection condition and then maps out what a cosmological constant changes. It is a chapter of negative results, and the negative results are informative: they show that any join of the two pieces must be dynamical, which motivates the junction analysis of the next two chapters.

\section{Static solutions with \texorpdfstring{$w=r'$}{w=r'}}

Take a static metric $-N(s)^2\dd t^2+\dd s^2+r(s)^2\dd\Omega^2$ in proper-distance gauge, the magnetic ansatz with $w=r'$, and write $V=w'=r''$. With $\rho$ and $p_r$ normalised as $8\pi G\times$ the physical quantities,
\[
\rho=2\ell^2\Bigl(\frac{V^2}{r^2}+\frac{(1-w^2)^2}{2r^4}\Bigr),\qquad p_r=2\ell^2\Bigl(\frac{V^2}{r^2}-\frac{(1-w^2)^2}{2r^4}\Bigr),
\]
the static field equations used are
\begin{align*}
\text{(a)}\quad&\frac{1-w^2-2rV}{r^2}=\rho &&\text{(Hamiltonian constraint)},\\
\text{(b)}\quad&\frac{2w}{r}\frac{N'}{N}+\frac{w^2-1}{r^2}=p_r &&\text{(radial Einstein equation)},\\
\text{(d)}\quad&(Nw')'=\frac{Nw(w^2-1)}{r^2} &&\text{(Yang--Mills equation)}.
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[Static classification]\label{thm:staticclass}\ver\nogo\
On an interval where $r$ and $N$ do not vanish, every solution of (a), (b), (d) with $w=r'$ has either $w\equiv0$, in which case $r^2\equiv\ell^2$ (the Bertotti--Robinson throat), or $w\equiv\pm1$, in which case $V\equiv0$ and $\rho\equiv0$ (flat space). In particular no static solution joins the throat to anything else.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This proof is a sketch: the final polynomial identity is too long to verify by hand.

\emph{A flow without the lapse.} With $w=r'$ and $V=w'$, equation (d) reads $N'V+NV'=Nw(w^2-1)/r^2$. Where $w\ne0$, equation (b) gives
\[
\frac{N'}N=\frac r{2w}\Bigl(p_r+\frac{1-w^2}{r^2}\Bigr).
\]
Dividing (d) by $N$ and inserting this,
\[
V'=\frac{w(w^2-1)}{r^2}-\frac{rV}{2w}\Bigl(p_r+\frac{1-w^2}{r^2}\Bigr),
\]
in which $N$ no longer appears. Together with $r'=w$ and $w'=V$ this is a vector field on the space of $(r,w,V)$.

\emph{The certificate.} Multiply (a) by $r^4$ to obtain the polynomial
\[
E_0=r^2(1-w^2-2rV)-2\ell^2\bigl(r^2V^2+\tfrac12(1-w^2)^2\bigr).
\]
On an open set where (a) holds and $w(w^2-1)\ne0$, the derivatives $E_1=E_0'$, $E_2=E_1'$ and $E_3=E_2'$ along the flow vanish too. After clearing the denominator $w$, each is a polynomial in $(\ell,r,w,V)$. The identity
\[
\ell^2\bigl(rw(w^2-1)\bigr)^4=k_0E_0+k_1E_1+k_2E_2+k_3E_3 ,
\]
with explicit polynomial coefficients $k_i$, is checked in Lean by expanding both sides. It was found with a Gr\"obner basis and a Macaulay matrix of degree $16$, and it is too long to check by hand. It shows that the open set is empty.

\emph{Conclusion.} So at every point $w\in\{0,1,-1\}$. Since $w$ is continuous on an interval, it is constant. If $w\equiv0$, then $V=0$ and (a) gives $1/r^2=\ell^2/r^4$, that is, $r^2=\ell^2$. If $w\equiv\pm1$, then $V=w'=0$ and $\rho=0$.
\end{proof}


\section{A cosmological constant: the map of smoothness}

Now relax the spin-connection condition and add a cosmological constant $\Lambda$. Work in units $\ell=1$ and write the static metric as $-e^{2\nu}\dd t^2+\dd s^2+r(s)^2\dd\Omega^2$, with $\kappa\rho=2w'^2/r^2+(1-w^2)^2/r^4$, $\kappa p_s=2w'^2/r^2-(1-w^2)^2/r^4$ and $\kappa p_\perp=(1-w^2)^2/r^4$.

\begin{theorem}[No static waist]\label{thm:nowaist}\ver\nogo\
At a point where $r'=0$, the $tt$ and $ss$ components of the field equations give $r''=-2w'^2/r\le0$. A static configuration cannot have a minimal sphere (a waist); the limiting case $r''=0$ is a cylinder.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a point with $r'=0$ the two equations are
\[
2rr''=1-r^2(\kappa\rho+\Lambda),\qquad-\frac1{r^2}+\Lambda-\kappa p_s=0 .
\]
The second gives $\Lambda=1/r^2+\kappa p_s$. Insert it into the first:
\[
2rr''=-r^2\kappa(\rho+p_s)=-r^2\cdot\frac{4w'^2}{r^2}=-4w'^2 .
\]
So $r''=-2w'^2/r\le0$, and the sphere cannot be a strict local minimum of $r$.
\end{proof}


This is the static form of the statement that a throat requires a violation of the null energy condition, and Yang--Mills fields do not provide one.

\begin{theorem}[Cylinders, a static universe and the critical cylinder]\label{thm:cylinders}\ver\
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A cylinder $r=R$, $w=0$, $\nu'=k$ constant solves the equations if and only if $\Lambda=1/R^2-1/R^4$ and $k^2=(2-R^2)/R^4$. For $k^2>0$ it is $\mathrm{AdS}_2\times S^2$, for $k=0$ it is $\mathrm{Mink}_2\times S^2$. Cylinders exist only for $\Lambda\le\frac14$, with equality iff $R^2=2$. At $\Lambda=0$, $R=1=\ell$: the Bertotti--Robinson throat.
\item The static universe $r=\sqrt2\sin(s/\sqrt2)$, $w=\cos(s/\sqrt2)$, $\nu=0$, $\Lambda=\frac34$ is an exact solution of all field equations.
\item On the critical cylinder $R^2=2$, $\Lambda=\frac14$, the linearised Yang--Mills equation is $w''+w/2=0$, solved by $w=\cos(s/\sqrt2)$, the same function as in (ii).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} For $w=0$ and $r=R$ we have $\kappa\rho=\kappa p_\perp=1/R^4$ and $\kappa p_s=-1/R^4$, and $r'=r''=0$.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The $tt$ equation reads $0=1-R^2(1/R^4+\Lambda)$, so $\Lambda=1/R^2-1/R^4$. The $ss$ equation gives the same condition.
\item The $\theta\theta$ equation reads $\nu''+\nu'^2=\kappa p_\perp-\Lambda$, that is, $k^2=1/R^4-\Lambda=(2-R^2)/R^4$.
\item The Yang--Mills equation is satisfied trivially by $w=0$.
\end{itemize}
With $u=1/R^2$, $\Lambda=u-u^2=\frac14-(u-\frac12)^2\le\frac14$, with equality exactly at $u=\frac12$, that is, $R^2=2$. At $\Lambda=0$, $u=1$, so $R=1$ and $k=1$.

\emph{(ii)} Put $x=s/\sqrt2$. Then $r=\sqrt2\sin x$, $r'=\cos x$ and $r''=-\sin x/\sqrt2$, while $w=\cos x$ and $w'=-\sin x/\sqrt2$. From these,
\[
\frac{2w'^2}{r^2}=\frac12,\qquad\frac{(1-w^2)^2}{r^4}=\frac14,
\]
so $\kappa\rho=\frac34$, $\kappa p_s=\frac14$ and $\kappa p_\perp=\frac14$. Each equation is now one line.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $tt$: $2rr''=-2\sin^2x$, and $1-r'^2-r^2(\frac34+\frac34)=\sin^2x-3\sin^2x$.
\item $ss$: $-\frac{1-r'^2}{r^2}+\frac34-\frac14=-\frac12+\frac12=0$.
\item $\theta\theta$: with $\nu=0$ it reads $\frac{r''}r=\kappa p_\perp-\Lambda$, and both sides equal $-\frac12$.
\item Yang--Mills: $w''=-\frac{\cos x}2$, and $\frac{w(w^2-1)}{r^2}=\frac{-\cos x\sin^2x}{2\sin^2x}$ is the same.
\end{itemize}

\emph{(iii)} On the critical cylinder $k=0$, so $\nu$ is constant. The Yang--Mills equation is $w''=w(w^2-1)/R^2$, and its linear part at $w=0$ is $w''=-w/2$. Then $(\cos(s/\sqrt2))''=-\frac12\cos(s/\sqrt2)$.
\end{proof}


The critical cylinder is the ultracold limit of the magnetically charged Reissner--Nordstr\"om--de Sitter solution, in which three horizons coincide; it is the Plebański--Hacyan spacetime \cite{PlebanskiHacyan1979}. Between the static universe at $\Lambda=\frac34$ and the critical cylinder at $\Lambda=\frac14$ there is a sequence of static closed solutions found numerically by Volkov and collaborators \cite{Volkov1996} \ext.

\section{Inside the throat: an oscillating Yang--Mills field}

The throat itself is stable as a geometry, but the Yang--Mills field inside it is not. Linearise around $w=0$ in the throat of radius $\ell=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Throat mode]\label{thm:throatmode}\ver\
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item In the throat, static perturbations satisfy $w''+w'+w=0$, solved by $w=e^{-s/2}\cos(\sqrt3\,s/2)$.
\item The effective mass squared is $-1$, below the Breitenlohner--Freedman bound $-\frac14$ of $\mathrm{AdS}_2$, and the oscillation frequency is $\sqrt{1-\frac14}=\sqrt3/2$. Between consecutive nodes, a distance $2\pi/\sqrt3$ apart, the amplitude decreases by $e^{-\pi/\sqrt3}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(i)} In the throat $R=1$ and $\nu'=k=1$. The static Yang--Mills equation is $e^{-\nu}(e^\nu w')'=w(w^2-1)/R^2$, that is, $w''+w'=w(w^2-1)$. Its linear part at $w=0$ is $w''+w'+w=0$. The characteristic equation $\lambda^2+\lambda+1=0$ has roots $-\frac12\pm\frac{\sqrt3}2\ii$, which gives the stated solution.

To check it directly, write $c=\cos(\sqrt3s/2)$ and $\sigma=\sin(\sqrt3s/2)$. Then
\[
w'=e^{-s/2}\Bigl(-\tfrac12c-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\sigma\Bigr),\qquad w''=e^{-s/2}\Bigl(-\tfrac12c+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\sigma\Bigr),
\]
and $w''+w'+w=e^{-s/2}(-\frac12-\frac12+1)c=0$.

\emph{(ii)} Write the linear equation as $w''+w'-m^2w=0$; comparing with (i) gives $m^2=-1$. Substituting $w=e^{-s/2}u$ removes the first-derivative term and leaves $u''-(m^2+\frac14)u=0$. The solutions oscillate, rather than grow or decay exponentially, exactly when $m^2<-\frac14$, which is the Breitenlohner--Freedman criterion. Here $-(m^2+\frac14)=\frac34$. The angular frequency is $\sqrt{3/4}$, so consecutive zeros of the cosine are $\pi/(\sqrt3/2)=2\pi/\sqrt3$ apart. Over this distance the factor $e^{-s/2}$ decreases by $e^{-\pi/\sqrt3}$.
\end{proof}


The same number $e^{-\pi/\sqrt3}$ governs the approach of the Bartnik--McKinnon solutions to the extremal Reissner--Nordstr\"om geometry: each further node of the gauge field adds a throat segment and reduces the mass deficit by this factor \cite{BFM1994} \num. The instability of $w=0$ in the throat is the reason the throat alone is not a stable Yang--Mills configuration; we return to it in Chapter~22.

\begin{litbox}
Static spherically symmetric EYM solutions are the subject of a large literature, beginning with Bartnik and McKinnon \cite{BartnikMcKinnon1988} and reviewed by Volkov and Gal'tsov \cite{VolkovGaltsov1999}; the scaling $e^{-\pi/\sqrt3}$ is due to Breitenlohner, Forg\'acs and Maison \cite{BFM1994}; cosmological generalisations are due to Volkov et al.\ \cite{Volkov1996}; the Breitenlohner--Freedman bound is from \cite{BreitenlohnerFreedman1982}. New here are the classification of \cref{thm:staticclass} under the spin-connection condition, which leaves only the throat and flat space and is proved with an explicit algebraic certificate, and the compact statement of \cref{thm:nowaist}. Together with the explicit cylinder family and exact static universe they form a ``map'' of where static smooth configurations exist.
\end{litbox}

\section*{What this chapter passes on}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item No static solution with $w=r'$ joins the throat to anything else (\cref{thm:staticclass}).
\item No static waist: $r''=-2w'^2/r\le0$ (\cref{thm:nowaist}).
\item Cylinders exist iff $\Lambda\le1/(4\ell^2)$; exact static universe at $\Lambda=3/(4\ell^2)$ (\cref{thm:cylinders}).
\item The throat mode violates the BF bound (\cref{thm:throatmode}).
\end{itemize}
