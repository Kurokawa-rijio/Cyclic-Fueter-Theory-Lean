## Overview

Cyclic Fueter Theory develops a geometric construction beginning with cyclic quaternionic Fueter-type operators and their complex matrix and spinor representations. This Lean 4 archive formalizes selected calculations that connect a momentum-space quantum sphere and Dirac-type operators to relativistic mass-shell geometry, then studies a spherical Einstein–Yang–Mills (EYM) dumbbell whose gauge profile is tied to its spatial shape. The purpose is to make the algebraic identities, geometric correspondences, and field-equation calculations auditable—not to present a completed physical unification.

Version 0.5.0 contains 1641 theorem declarations in 150 modules (a count of declarations, not of independent claims), plus 340 aliases in an index module that restate existing theorems under ledger names. All of them build without `sorry` or project-specific axioms. The program is summarised by one sentence: the dumbbell is kinematics; putting in an action produces dynamics. The claim ledger records each item in that form.

## Quantum sphere and relativistic geometry

For mass m > 0, define E = √(m² + |p|²) and Q(p) = (m/E, p/E). The formalization verifies that Q is a bijection from momentum space to the open upper hemisphere of the unit three-sphere and computes its pullback metric, ∂ᵢQ·∂ⱼQ = δᵢⱼ/E² − pᵢpⱼ/E⁴.

For the Dirac Hamiltonian H = mβ + α·p, the Clifford relations yield H² = E²; the positive-energy projector P₊ = (1 + H/E)/2 is shown to be idempotent. Its quantum metric equals one half of the metric induced by Q, corresponding in these conventions to a sphere of radius 1/√2. The code also checks quaternion-to-complex-matrix identities and the determinant/Lorentz structure of the SL(2,ℂ) action.

The same momentum variables parameterize the relativistic mass-shell hyperboloid (E/m, p/m). The code verifies reciprocal-coordinate and Gudermann-angle identities, as well as conformal-metric, slope, normal, and area relations between the hyperbolic mass shell and the quantum hemisphere. These are precise geometric correspondences under the stated definitions, not by themselves proof of physical equivalence between relativity and quantum theory.

## Fueter, Berry, and Wigner connections

For U(p) = (m − iσ·p)/E, the code computes U⁻¹dU, verifies its Maurer–Cartan identity, and checks the induced frame metric. It compares the Fueter/frame connection A_F = ½U⁻¹dU, the Berry connection A_B, and the Wigner connection A_W. With the sign and normalization conventions in the source,

A_B = −(m/E) A_W,  𝒢_F = 𝒢_B = (m/E)⁴ 𝒢_W,

where 𝒢 is the encoded trace-Gram invariant of the curvature. For E > m > 0, these invariant values distinguish the Fueter/frame and Berry connections from the Wigner connection; the source includes an explicit proof that the Berry and Wigner connections cannot be gauge-equivalent in this regime. Other modules formalize selected Foldy–Wouthuysen identities, the SU(2)-coupled Dirac operator on S³ (reduced from the left-invariant frame and spin connection to D = 3/2 + λΣσ⊗σ on the invariant subspace, with levels 3/2 + λ and 3/2 − 3λ), a heat-kernel coefficient, and monopole Dirac zero-mode formulas; some spectral results use stated external theorems.

## The Einstein–Yang–Mills dumbbell

The second construction is a time-symmetric, spherically symmetric spatial geometry ds² + r(s)² dΩ², with spherical caps r(s) = ℓ sin(s/ℓ) joined to a cylindrical neck r(s) = ℓ, locally modeled on S² × ℝ. In the magnetic spherical SU(2) Yang–Mills ansatz, W = r′ ties the gauge profile to the geometry, and the Hamiltonian constraint fixes the common scale ℓ² = 4πG/g².

Field equations from the action. The first variation of the reduced Einstein–Hilbert + Gibbons–Hawking–York + Yang–Mills action reproduces the reduced field equations. In version 0.2.0, all 16 components of the four-dimensional Einstein tensor (computed from the Levi-Civita formula) and all 12 components of the Yang–Mills equations (with F = dA + A∧A) are computed for the spherical ansatz in synchronous gauge and shown to be equivalent to the same equations, so Palais' principle of symmetric criticality is not needed for this ansatz.

Junctions. Junction conditions are derived from the field equations read as distributions, using a δ-coefficient lemma proved by integration by parts and assuming each side is C² up to the surface with matching traces. For the dumbbell, a timelike junction surface requires both a gravitational shell and a colour surface current; a spacelike slice has shell density σ = −[r′]/(4πG r₀); a null front carries neither a shell nor a colour current, but carries a weak discontinuity [W_u] that is constant along the front. Correction in v0.3.0: the final theorems that pass from the differential-field identities to real functions use a structure (`RealLine`) in which a field with continuous ring-homomorphism evaluations forces every element to be constant; these final theorems therefore apply only to constant fields. The identities and the real-function lemmas are unaffected, and the corresponding four ledger items are classified as partial until the bridge is rebuilt on a commutative ring.

Evolution. Outside the fronts the solution is exact: each cap is a closed radiation FRW universe and the neck is AdS₂ × S². The wedge between the fronts is a characteristic initial-value (Goursat) problem; the constraints are proved to propagate, while local existence and uniqueness are cited as classical theorems. Along the cap centre, a(η) = ℓ cos η and the Kretschmann scalar K = 24ℓ⁴/a⁸ diverges at central proper time ℓ < πℓ/2. The neck does not collapse; its synchronous time πℓ/2 marks the edge of the domain of dependence, not a curvature singularity. The minimal mass m ≥ √π M_Pl/g is derived from the constraints, an untrapped exterior, the dominant energy condition, and the neck's field equation.

The archive does not claim a global continuation of the solution in the wedge, and W = r′ is a condition on initial data, not an established dynamically invariant sector of the full EYM system.

## Quantum cosmology of the neck and quantum stress on the caps

Since version 0.3.0. The reduced Lagrangian of the neck is derived from the four-dimensional action, with the total derivative cancelled exactly by the Gibbons–Hawking–York term and the Legendre map shown to be regular. For the Wheeler–DeWitt operator the WKB series terminates at first order in ħ, so Ψ = e^{iaσ/ħ}/σ (σ² = Cb − 4c²(b² + ℓ²)) is an exact solution where σ² > 0, for both momentum branches and both the "momenta to the right" and Laplace–Beltrami orderings. The physical inner product is fixed, up to unitary equivalence, by the affine-group quantisation of the reduced phase space; the semiclassical limit verifies the hypotheses of the stationary-phase theorem. On the caps, for conformal spectator fields in the conformal vacuum, the trace anomaly and conservation determine the energy density ρ = κ₁/a⁴ + (3ħa_E/8π²)X² (X = (ȧ² + 1)/a²) uniquely up to a radiation term; the ultrastatic S³ is shown to be incompatible with traceless Yang–Mills stress without a cosmological constant. The back-reaction of the SU(2) fluctuations themselves, whose trace contains a β-function term, is listed as an open item.

## New in version 0.5.0

**Bridges (α-X.9, X.12, X.13).** *Common clock:* in Gaussian normal time from the initial slice the cap's crunch (central proper time ℓ) and the neck's focal time (πℓ/2) are distinct events; an earlier identification η = t/ℓ is a coordinate coincidence (`CFT.BridgeClock.two_events`). *Twist:* with W = r′, the spin connection and the gauge field cancel on the spin–isospin singlet; with an adjoint Yukawa coupling the zero modes are exp(∓∫m) and localise one on each cap (external: conformal covariance of the Dirac operator, the Callias index; the reduction of the 4D Dirac–Yukawa system to f′ = −img, g′ = imf is not formalised). *Neck temperature:* the JT dilaton selects a Killing vector; an invariant splits the cases into three classes; a² − 1/ℓ² = (2πT_loc)² (Deser–Levin form).

**Root review (α-X.14, I.8).** The cap–neck junction is C¹ but not C²: ³R jumps from 6/ℓ² to 2/ℓ², the energy density by a factor 3, and the radial pressure changes sign. The Hamiltonian constraint is quadratic in r″ and has exactly the two roots 0 (neck) and −1/ℓ (cap) at the junction, so gluing the exact cap and neck solutions cannot be C² (a general C² statement would need ODE uniqueness, not formalised). *Duality:* for M(z) = z₀ + i z·σ, det M = z₀² + z·z; the real slice is SU(2) (S³), the imaginary slice is Hermitian with Minkowski determinant (H³); the quantum hemisphere is the hemisphere model of H³ and its equator lies at the hyperboloid's infinity.

**New pillar (α-XII): polar decomposition and the self-dual connection.** Every element of SL(2,ℂ) is uniquely a rotation (SU(2)) times a boost (positive Hermitian), with existence and uniqueness proved. The sphere and the hyperboloid meet only at the identity. With A = Γ + iK the cap has c = 1 + iȧ, the curvature coefficient c² − 2c = −|c|² is real, Friedmann ⇒ |c|² = (8πG/3)ρa², and the phase is conformal time. The neck is rotation (S², monopole) × boost (AdS₂). On the exact cap and neck the Yang–Mills field equals the real part of A at all times (test B, restricted to these solutions; in general evolution "YM = Re A" is a postulate). The cap's self-dual connection has the form of the v6 mass shell and Gudermann angle (the physical reading is marked as interpretation). The "phase clock" (XII.5) is classified as partial, since in Gaussian time the end events differ (X.9).

**Conformal frame (α-XIII).** The cap Ω = ℓ cos η and the neck Ω = ℓ/sin u are conformal multiples of the Einstein static universe ℝ × S³, and both solve □̂Ω = Ω (the trace of the Einstein equations with traceless Yang–Mills stress; the conformal-transformation formula is external). The junction jump is a seam in Ω″. The ηη components give ℓ² = 4πG/g² on both. Minisuperspace: S = (3π/4G)∫(Ω² − Ω̇²)dη, every solution has period 2π, and the energy balance with the Yang–Mills energy 3π²/g² is equivalent to ℓ² = 4πG/g². A semiclassical quantisation gives the candidate ℓ²/ℓ_P² = 4(n + ½)/(3π), α = 3π/(4n + 2). Operator ordering, quantisation of the Yang–Mills field and the minisuperspace truncation are not assessed, so this is classified as partial. Numerically, a static conformally flat smooth interpolation fails (partial). With Weyl curvature, the Bartnik–McKinnon sequence approaches a smooth core plus an infinitely long throat of radius exactly ℓ (external: Breitenlohner–Forgács–Maison; numerics reproduce b_n = 0.4537, 0.6517, 0.6970, 0.7048, 0.7062).

**Smoothness map (α-XIV).** *No static throat:* in static EYM + Λ, at r′ = 0 one has r″ = −2W′²/r ≤ 0. *Cylinders (necks) with W = 0:* Λ = 1/R² − 1/R⁴ ≤ 1/(4ℓ²), with equality iff R = √2ℓ (the ultracold limit of magnetic Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter, Minkowski₂ × S²). Λ = 0 gives AdS₂ × S² of radius ℓ. *Einstein static universe + Yang–Mills* is an exact solution with radius √2ℓ and Λ = 3/(4ℓ²). *Closed smooth static solutions* (cosmological Bartnik–McKinnon analogues; external: Volkov et al. 1996, hep-th/9605089) are reproduced numerically up to n = 10, agreeing to six digits for n ≤ 5. They are cigar-shaped, the middle approaches the √2ℓ cylinder, and the length added per step approaches π√2ℓ. The literature reports these static solutions to be unstable. *Throat:* in the AdS₂ throat the Yang–Mills mode violates the Breitenlohner–Freedman bound (e^{−s/2}cos(√3 s/2)); numerically the BM mass deficit scales as 1 − M_n ∝ e^{−πn/√3} and the throat grows by π/√3 ℓ per step. *Negative results:* the sum of the cap and neck conformal factors violates the null energy condition near the poles (theorem for the null–null component) and cannot be realised by a Yang–Mills field elsewhere (numerics). The relation between the classical node number and the quantum n is open.

## Scope and reproducibility

Lean checks each theorem from its declared hypotheses and imported results. This archive provides machine-checked algebraic, differential, metric, curvature, distributional, spectral-formula, and constraint calculations; it is not a numerical PDE solver and does not claim a completed unification of general relativity and quantum theory. An author-maintained ledger classifies 134 items as 62 complete, 46 complete using a named external theorem or literature, 9 complete in a differential field pending the bridge to real functions, 10 partial, and 7 not started. Numerical results quoted in the ledger are not verified by Lean. `DepCheck.lean` records which lemmas each final theorem's proof uses.

The project targets Lean 4.15.0 and Mathlib v4.15.0. To reproduce the build, install Elan and Git, then run `lake update`, `lake exe cache get`, and `lake build` from the repository root; `lake env lean Audit.lean` reproduces the axiom audit. The English README and the Japanese status ledger (docs/CLAIM_STATUS_JA.md) describe each module and claim.
